# ProcedureRoute

A procedure route draws on the map a published departure (SID) or arrival (STAR): from the runway to its last point, or from its first point to the runway (Q56, 16/10).

**Drawn on demand.** Pressing a place chip of a procedure ("ELCOB 7G") draws its whole route, in place of the ring the chip showed alone; it stays until it is closed or another one is asked for (a route takes longer than ten seconds to read). The sky's note says what it is and closes it: "ELCOB 7G · departure from 26R · published route" and its ✕ (`cl-sky__note--action`).

**The line.** `color-map-procedure` (`#e0e0e0`, `color-map-light-procedure` on IGN and OSM), 1.6 px, solid; chevrons every 46 px in the direction flown (outward for a SID, toward the runway for a STAR); the runway's designator at its start or end. When the tables give the doublet ("RWY26L-26R") and the runways in use do not say which of the two, both first segments are drawn and the note says "departure from 26L or 26R". It is a reference, not a path: neither the aircraft's altitude colour nor the accent, which stays the followed aircraft's own track. When the aircraft is identified and followed, both show: the route in neutral, its track in the accent, dashed; the gap between them is the controller's direct.

**The points.** Named reporting points and navaids as small hollow triangles (5 px), their name beside in mono 10 px `color-map-procedure`, on the side of the point away from the line: a name or a constraint is never crossed by a route; the technical terminal points (PG271…) are not drawn, the line passes through them. Under the name, its constraints when the table gives them and they read without doubt (a doubtful one is left out, never guessed), in mono 9.5 px `color-text-tertiary`, in the brand's numbers: "≥ FL 060", "≤ 4 000 ft", "≤ 250 kt", "FL 070" when it is exact.

**The layer** (MapControls › Layers): "Procedures (SID, STAR)", off by default; when on, a `SegmentedControl` "Arrivals · Departures" (arrivals by default: the approach is what is heard), and the configuration it draws, said and not chosen: "LFPG west (26L, 27R), from the runways in use". It follows the runways in use already judged from the ADS-B tracks (the direction rule): no selector to keep in sync; while they are not known, nothing is drawn and the row says "Shown once the runway in use is known." The layer's routes take `color-map-procedure` at 38 %, 1.1 px, chevrons every 70 px, their named points at 60 %; the route asked for from a chip draws above them at full strength. Point names follow the labels' tiers: named up to 67 NM across the sky, triangles alone beyond; aircraft and their labels draw above the routes.

**Words.** The help of the chip, the note and the layer says what a route is: "The published route of ELCOB 7G, a departure from 26R. The controller often clears an aircraft direct to a later point: the route is what was published, not the path flown." The layer's row says it in general: "Published routes: the controller often clears an aircraft direct to a later point, so a route is not the path flown." A route is drawn whole or not at all: a point missing from the tables, or a designation heard that the cycle's tables do not have ("ATREX 7K" when they give 6G and 6K), and the chip shows its point alone, its help ending "ATREX 7K is not in this cycle's tables: its route is not drawn. Click to see ATREX on the map."; the variant is never guessed. Outside the French eAIP nothing is drawn: the chip of a procedure shows its point alone when it is known, the layer's row has no switch and says "Not for this station: published routes come from the French eAIP (aerodromes LF…).", as the source lines of Regional data.

**Data.** The coding tables of the eAIP (SID and STAR), per procedure: its points in order with their position, its runways, its kind, and per segment the altitude and speed constraints.
