# MapLabel

A map label names an aircraft on the map: phase, callsign and type; its level and speed; and, for ten minutes after it was heard, what it last said.

**Markup.** `<span class="cl-maplabel">` with up to three lines: `<span class="cl-maplabel__ph--<family>">APP</span> <b>AFR47KX</b> A320`; the level, its trend and the ground speed ("2 400 ft ↓ 158 kt"); and, apart from the label, the message tag (below). `is-selected` adds the accent outline. The icon is drawn by the map renderer from our aircraft family (assets › Aircraft), its fill on the altitude ramp (`color-altitude-dark-*`: pale yellow near the ground, then pink, violet, cyan, slate at cruise).

**Signal lost.** An aircraft whose position the receiver no longer gets stays where it was last seen, for the usual ten minutes. Its glyph is drawn **as an outline** (1.5 px, `color-text-tertiary`, no fill), the shape of the status light's hollow ○: grey alone sits only ΔE 14 from the cruise slate of the altitude ramp, too close for a small glyph. Its label goes to `color-text-tertiary`, the phase prefix too (it has no current phase; the code stays written), and its past track ("Tracks, last N min") in `color-text-tertiary` as well, ending under the glyph; the second line reads "signal lost 3 min · last 1 620 ft" (the brand's numbers: thin space, "FL 110" above the transition level), in place of level, trend and speed; no age counter. It reaches its last position by the relocation fade of the motion engine (out at the estimate, in at the last fix), never by sliding back: the motion never runs backwards (D80). No dashed segment is drawn: the glyph sits on its last point, the track ends there.

**Rules.** The label carries the phase colour (its prefix), the icon the altitude ramp: the two palettes are kept apart by measure (ΔE ≥ 5 under every vision). The last transmission is not a third line any more (owner, 15/10): it widened the label to 40 mono characters and read as data; it is the message tag below. A click on the aircraft opens its story. The aircraft that just spoke wears a green ring that fades over 10 s.

**Zoom tiers** (owner, 15/10). Measured on the test instance's real sky (about 110 aircraft): at the default view, 34 NM across the sky, 62 % of full labels overlap another and half cover another aircraft; at 67 NM, 87 %; callsigns alone still overlap 47 to 87 % beyond 67 NM. The tier follows the **width of the sky in NM** (resolution × width), not the zoom level, since folding the column widens the sky:
- up to 24 NM across: the full label (two lines) for every aircraft;
- from 24 to 67 NM: the callsign alone (one line, mono 11 px, primary, no phase prefix, no age), just above the glyph; the aircraft that matter keep the full label;
- beyond 67 NM: only the aircraft that matter, by their callsign alone; the others are their glyph;
- hovering an aircraft always shows its full label.
The aircraft that matter: heard in the last ten minutes, in an arrival sequence, selected or followed, squawking 7500, 7600 or 7700.

**Message tag** (owner, 15/10), the last transmission matched to the aircraft, wherever its full label shows: a box hung 3 px under the label, left edges aligned; background `color-surface-raised`; a 2.5 px bar on its left in `color-status-info` (what was heard, as the radio dot of the story); the words in the text font, 11 px, `color-text-primary`, in quotes, cut at 34 characters with "…"; the message's age at the right in mono 10 px `color-text-tertiary` ("12 s", "3 min"). Full for two minutes, at 55 % from two to ten, gone at ten; the setting "Last transmission on aircraft labels" (key `voiceBubbles`) turns it off. The label and its tag move up together, the tag ending 2 px above the glyph's box (the two-line label alone ends about 7 px into it, its translucent ground letting the glyph show; the opaque tag would hide it). Delivered in migration cad595f; measured on one sky at one instant: boxes overlapping another went from an estimated 67 % to 37 % at the default 34 NM, from 81 % to 20 % at 67 NM, and the aircraft under someone else's label from 28 to 5 at 67 NM.

**Probable runway and exit** (Q57, proposed 16/10; the owner decides, since it shows an inference). What the aircraft's own ADS-B track says of where it is going, at the end of the second line, two spaces after the speed, in mono 11 px `color-text-secondary`: "3 000 ft ↓ 182 kt  →26L". The arrow reads "heading for"; the uncertainty is written in the content, not in a style:
- an arrival, from 20 NM out (where the arrival sequence's cone starts): its pair of runways, "→26L/R", sure from the first prediction (measured on 2 338 arrivals, every error was the other runway of the pair); the runway itself, "→26L", from 15 NM (right 96.6 times in 100 at LFPG) or at once where the airport has no pair (LFPO: 100 %). Nothing beyond 20 NM: a label speaks of its aircraft, and the configuration in use is already said in Right now;
- a departure, from 15 NM out (before, the true exit is in the list only 52 times in 100): the most likely exit and how many others stay possible, the count in `color-text-tertiary`: "→OKASI +2", then "+1" as the list is pruned (exits under 10 % dropped), then "→OKASI" alone once its margin over the second reaches 10° (93 times in 100 at 20 NM, for half the departures). Nothing for an aircraft guided on a heading: it aims at no exit. Gone once it passes its exit;
- no "?" (every arrival within 20 NM would carry one), no italic (a slanted mono at 11 px on the canvas reads worse), no opacity (it already means set aside or signal lost), no percentage (the top of the scale is under-estimated: a "65 %" is right 91 times in 100 at 15 NM).
Only in the full label (its tiers, and hovering); the callsign-only label carries nothing. The setting "Probable runway and exit" (Map controls › Aircraft) turns it off.
